# Derby Day

A browser football club manager where you also play the matches yourself, mouse-first. You run a generated club (squad, transfers, training, an Elo ladder) and play eleven-a-side matches in real time against the CPU, under the Laws of the Game: throw-ins, goal kicks, corners, offside, fouls, free kicks, penalties and cards.

This is a prototype. The plan for turning it into an online live-service game is in [docs/ROADMAP.md](docs/ROADMAP.md).

## Play

```sh
bun run build      # writes dist/index.html, a single self-contained file
open dist/index.html
```

Or `bun run dev` to rebuild on save and serve on http://localhost:5173.

Install Bun with `curl -fsSL https://bun.sh/install | bash` (no sudo needed). There are no npm dependencies.

### Controls

Mouse is the main scheme: your player runs to the pointer, and **the pointer is also where every kick goes**. No sprint key: the further the pointer is from your player, the faster you run: nothing inside 0.6 m, a walk just outside, a jog (about 3.7 m/s) at 4 m, normal top speed at 8 m, a flat-out sprint from 11 m out (sprinting costs stamina; when the bar goes red you are winded and can't sprint until it refills; it refills faster the slower you go, even at a run).

| | Mouse (recommended) | Keyboard |
|---|---|---|
| Run | Move the pointer. Distance sets speed: walk, jog (4 m), run (8 m), sprint (11 m+) | WASD or arrows, Shift to sprint |
| Pass (with the ball) | Left click: always a ground pass. Firm, to the feet of the teammate at or toward the pointer (or where he is running to); on empty grass it rolls out to the point | Space (to the teammate you are facing) |
| Lofted ball (with the ball) | Press and hold left: the charge shows from 0.3 s and is full at 0.9 s; release and it lands at the pointer. A longer hold goes higher and further (12 m range at the shortest, 40 m full). The teammate nearest where it comes down is its receiver: he goes to where it drops and takes it on the chest or thigh. At a corner or a free kick it lands where left went down at any range, the hold picking driven or floated, and a sideways drag swings it | Hold C, release |
| Shoot (with the ball) | Right click: a low shot at the pointer. Hold right for power, release to strike: the harder you hit it the higher it rises (about 0.3 m at the goal line for a click, about 2 m at full power), and a poorer shooter's blast can go over. At a free kick it goes over the wall to where right went down; drag sideways while holding to curl it | Hold F, release |
| First time (a pass on its way to you) | Click before it arrives: left plays it on first time (to the teammate you clicked), right shoots first time; for a ball he will head, the click aims the header (left: to that teammate, right: at goal, the side you clicked). A second click before he plays it is kept for whoever gets it from him: a header across to a teammate, then his first-time shot. A click on the receiver himself (within 3 m) just means take it | Space |
| Keeper (with the ball) | He is yours: left click rolls or throws it out, hold left throws it high, right click punts it (30 m to 62 m with power). He may walk about his area with it; after six seconds (Law 12, a countdown shows) he plays it himself. Goal kicks are yours too | Space, hold C, hold F |
| Switch player (no ball) | Left click on or near a teammate (within 4 m of him, the nearest); left click elsewhere to take the nearest teammate in that direction (40 degree cone), or stay if nobody is. Your own man counts only for a click right on him (1.5 m), so a click a few metres off him means "that way". The switch skips a man still on the floor when one on his feet is nearly as close | E |
| Tackle (no ball) | Right click on (or near) the man with the ball: a standing tackle when he is within 2 m of you, a slide when he is further. Elsewhere it goes toward the pointer, a slide beyond 2 m. A slide goes up to 4 m, wins or fouls when your feet reach the ball or you meet the man, and leaves you on the floor. A won slide leaves the ball at your feet and you up before the man you took it from, so your side comes away with it. Tackles can't be spammed: after a standing tackle you wait 0.75 s before the next, and after a slide 1 s once you are back up. A missed tackle leaves you down longer: about 0.4 s standing, 0.8 s from a slide, then a further 0.7 s or 1.5 s before you can go in again. With the pointer on the man with the ball (within 1.5 m of him or the ball) your man chases him at a run, and within 1.8 m of him, in front or to the side, your man goes in with a standing tackle with no click, whichever way the pointer is; from behind it stays a right click | Space |
| Pause | Esc or P | Esc or P |

**A click close to him.** He runs to the pointer, so it is rarely far from him, and a click within 4 m of him says which way to pass, not where: the ball goes, firm, to the teammate that way (within 30 degrees, then 55), the open one if there are two (nobody across the lane or at his shoulder), not too far off, or the way he faces if the click is right on him. A click on a teammate is still that teammate. Measured in scripted matches, clicks 1.5-3.5 m off the passer and up to 15 degrees out complete 79% of passes (35% before; a click right on the man: 80%).

**A pointer by the ball.** Your player stops just short of a still pointer, so it often ends up beside or behind the ball. A pass, shot or lofted ball aimed within 2.5 m of the ball has no direction to go on and is played the way he faces (the way the ball sits from him), never back through his legs; aim further away to play it back.

**Click or hold.** A press without the ball within 0.3 s of your own kick is the second half of a double click and is ignored, so a double click doesn't tap the pass away and a double right click after a shot doesn't slide. Any left press shorter than 300 ms is a click and a click is always a ground pass, never a lofted ball (360 ms on touch screens and pens, whose taps are slower). Real clicks last about 60-260 ms; the old 200 ms threshold turned slow clicks and trackpad taps into lofts. The pass goes on release, aimed where the button went down unless the pointer wandered more than 8 px meanwhile. A test clicks for 60-260 ms through the sim and gets a ground pass every time.

**Pass assist.** A teammate is the receiver when the pointer is within 1.5 m of him (or of where he is running to), plus 0.1 m per metre of pass up to 4 m (2.5 m at 10 m: the broadcast camera makes depth hard to judge), or when the pointer is on the line to him, within 12 degrees and 0.6 to 1.2 times his distance. Then the ball goes to his feet, played to arrive at 11 m/s for a short pass rising to 15 m/s from 15 m: a 10 m pass leaves the foot at 16 m/s and gets there in 0.7 s (firmer since playtesting: a scripted human clicking on the nearest open teammate completed 80% of passes, 89% now). He steps up to meet it. A click anywhere else is still a pass to someone (playtest): to the teammate nearest it (or where he is running to), to his feet, one in an offside position only if nobody else is; only with nobody of yours to play to does the ball go to the grass, slowing to 3.5 m/s at the point. A click well ahead of a runner, along his run (or more than 2 m beyond where a pass would meet him), or into the space ahead of a forward standing still, is a through ball: the assist tries the spot clicked and a few round it (across his run and along it) and plays the one where he beats the defenders by most, along the ground timed for his run, or chipped over them if only that wins; with nothing into space on, it is a firm pass to him where he is going. Measured in scripted matches on chances with the runner level with the last man and no defender near the space: 27% reached him (21% before), 43% one of ours (33%). A human's passes and lofted balls scatter about 40% as much as a CPU's.

**After a pass.** Control moves to the receiver as he gets it. While a pass is on its way to him he meets it: one played to him while he runs its way he lets catch him within about 0.3 s, checking his run (he used to hold his pace and run on away from a ball just behind him); a through ball, played into the space ahead of him, he takes in stride. An outfielder who receives the ball runs on toward the pointer at once. If control changed by itself otherwise (an auto-switch, your keeper's catch) and you have not moved the mouse since, he holds still (the keeper with the ball; an outfielder plays like the CPU would) instead of running back to where the pointer was left; move the mouse and he is yours again. A player you are already steering runs on when he takes a loose ball or a ball into space. While held, if a loose ball nobody is meant for is nearest him, he goes for it. The pointer that steers is smoothed (a One Euro filter: calm when still, no lag when moving fast); kicks use the exact point. A scripted human playing quick click passes to the nearest open teammate completes about 80% of them, about three passes a possession (it completed 29% on the old controls).

**Reaching the ball.** The man you steer reaches 1.1 m for an opponent's ball at his feet (a CPU player 0.65-0.95 m, by its pace), kills it 4 m/s faster than he otherwise could and reads it himself, so a pass that runs a metre from him is cut out. A loose ball whose path comes within 3 m of him he goes to meet, gently taking over from the pointer unless it is well away and sending him the other way, so a pointer a step off doesn't leave him watching it run past. A ball dropping through head height within 6 m of him, when he is his side's nearest, he runs to meet and heads: away toward the pointer when it is 4 m or more off, at goal the side of the striker the pointer is on picks the corner.

**The ball and bodies.** The ball meets players by height: below 0.5 m it is at their feet (controlled if it isn't too hot for them, else it comes off the leg; one that runs onto a man's middle is easier to kill than one he stretches for, up to 5 m/s more, except a CPU player cutting out a ball a human side played), up to 1.5 m it is taken on the thigh or chest (1.9 m from a teammate) and drops to the feet, up to 2.5 m it can be headed (reach 0.5-0.85 m by heading skill for the CPU, 0.9 m for the man you steer; it connects more often for a better header of the ball and less with opponents at his shoulder: clearances, and at goal in the box; when it is you, the side of the striker the pointer is on picks the side of the goal, about 0.65 m either side for a corner, so he stays on the ball's line), and anything that hits a body below 1.85 m bounces off it. A ball bouncing on grass keeps 65% of its pace across the ground, so a lofted pass sits up near where it lands. Nothing passes through a player. The ball a man is running with is at his feet, about a metre ahead, and further at a sprint (up to 0.6 m more for a poor dribbler, a fifth of that for a perfect one); an opponent on his feet that it runs into, or who steps across it, can poke it away without a tackle, more often the better he defends and the worse the carrier dribbles (straight into a standing man: about 3 in 4 between equals), so a carrier has to go round a man, not through him. A defender stretching for a ball that isn't at his body may miss it. The keeper in his area has hands. He reaches about 0.55-0.9 m around him standing, by quality, and up to 2.6 m high. He reacts 0.13-0.28 s after the ball last changed course and shuffles across toward its path (only part of the way for a quick strike, all of it when a shot from distance gives him most of a second), then dives late, timed to land as it arrives, across to where he reads it will pass; a better keeper dives further and faster and misreads it less. Down after a dive, his hands and body still lie along the line he went. He catches what isn't too hot (more easily into his body, less at full stretch, lying, or above 2 m). Otherwise he palms it back out (a poor keeper often into the six-yard box), tips it round the post or over the bar, never back between the posts. While he holds the ball nobody may barge him: opponents are kept 1.5 m away.

On screen (3D view): a light ring with a dark outline sits on the grass under the pointer. With the ball, a wedge runs from your player toward where a click would pass (at most 6.5 m long at normal zoom: the power is its fill, not its length), with a white reticle (circle and four ticks) at that spot and a ring round the receiver if there is one. The wedge is also the power gauge: it is lit from your player outward as far as the kick's power, in green (the sweet spot), amber (a firm pass, a shot past the sweet spot, or a loft that would fall short of the pointer) or red (over-hit), with the unlit rest as a faint white track; a plain click shows the pace it would pass at, and the power bar bottom right shows the same numbers (dimmer until you charge; the tick is where the sweet spot ends). While a left hold charges, dots also trace the lofted ball's real flight and a ring marks where it comes down. When the line from the ball through the pointer heads for their goal, a crosshair stands on the goal face where a right click's shot would cross the line, at the height it would cross (faint until you charge a shot, then in the power band's colour, dimmer when it would be wide or over). Without the ball, a yellow ring on a teammate shows who a left click would switch to. A click on a player's body (his head or chest, not only his feet) picks him. Your player has an orange triangle and his name overhead and a yellow ring on the ground; the small orange bars over your team are stamina (red when winded). A hand-picked player (a click that really switched) stays yours for 3 s before the auto-switch (to whoever is nearest the ball) may take him back; whoever gets the ball always becomes yours, your keeper included.

Phones: drag to run toward your finger (further is faster), tap **Pass** to pass, hold it to loft (the hold starts at 0.36 s); **Shoot** shoots, and without the ball it tackles; tap a player on the pitch to pick him. Replays recorded before this control scheme (format 1 codes) can't be played back and are refused politely. Format 4 codes carry the click's aim point and whether the pointer moved; format 3 codes still open but were recorded on an earlier engine, so the viewer says so. Replays from the six-a-side game (format 2) still open, padded to eleven a side, but play back a different match, and the viewer says it was recorded on a different version.

## The match: eleven-a-side under the Laws

A full-size pitch, 105 × 68 m, with the real markings (penalty area 16.5 m, goal area 5.5 m, penalty mark 11 m, centre circle and arcs 9.15 m, corner arcs) and goals 7.32 m by 2.44 m. There are no boards: the ball goes out of play. The constants are in `src/sim/constants.ts`, the Laws in `src/sim/rules.ts`.

- **Formations and mentality.** The Squad screen's tactics board picks one of nine shapes (4-4-2, 4-3-3, 4-2-3-1, 4-1-4-1, 4-4-1-1, 3-5-2, 3-4-3, 5-3-2, 5-4-1; the lineup board re-picks the XI for the new slots) and a mentality in five steps: Park the bus, Defensive, Balanced, Attacking, High press. Slots carry finer roles the CPU plays to: full-back, wing-back and centre-back; holding, central, attacking and wide midfielder; winger and striker. The mentality sets the CPU's knobs (`TACTICS` in `src/sim/ai.ts`): defensive line height, block width and depth, when the chaser goes in and how many press with him, counter-pressing after losing the ball, who commits forward, shot screening near the box, pass risk and tempo, how far full-backs and wing-backs go. When anyone on a side has the ball, two or three teammates offer short passes at different angles (two forward diagonals and one back inside, so there is always a triangle) and move again when an opponent closes the lane. Ladder clubs have their own formation and mentality, fixed per club and shown on the pre-match card. **Quick tactics:** the pause menu changes your side's mentality for the rest of the match (sent to the sim as `HumanInput.tactic` on the next tick, so replays play it back; the club's saved default is unchanged). From outside the box a Park the bus side shoots less and Attacking and High press sides more (`Tactic.shootLong`); every shot from beyond 16 m is harder to place (`LONG_SHOT_ERR` in `src/sim/constants.ts`), and `bun run balance` prints shots and goals by distance. `bun run scripts/tactics-probe.ts [matches]` measures what each mentality does against Balanced (line height, width, pressers, regain height, players forward, pass completion, fouls, goals).
- **Restarts (Laws 8, 15, 16, 17).** Kick-off, throw-in (from the hands at the touchline, can't score directly), goal kick (from the goal area, opponents outside the penalty area) and corner (from the arc, can score directly). Play stops, the ball is placed, everyone takes up a legal position (opponents 9.15 m away at free kicks and corners, 2 m at a throw-in), a taker walks up and plays it. When your side has the restart, the player taking it is yours: pass, loft or shoot as usual (a shot at a throw-in is a long throw, at a goal kick a long kick). A click while he walks to the ball is kept and played as soon as he is ready. Goal kicks are taken by your keeper, so they are yours too. Do nothing for five seconds and the CPU takes it for you. The kick-off is a kick like any other restart: the taker stands over the ball until he plays it (yours too: click to kick off), and nobody moves until he does. The taker may not play the ball again until someone else has touched it: if you do, it is an indirect free kick to the other side (the CPU never does), and a keeper may not pick up his own goal kick, free kick or release. The clock stops while the ball is dead (throw-ins, goal kicks, corners, free kicks, penalties and kick-offs) and starts again when the restart is taken. A CPU side's throw-in near the goal line it attacks is aimed at least 12 m short of that line, since a thrown ball comes down and runs on.
- **Ends and time (Laws 7, 8).** The teams change ends at half-time: the broadcast camera stays on the same long side, so in the second half you attack the other way. At 45:00 and 90:00 the board goes up (the clock shows `45:00 +2`) and the added time is played with the clock held: a minute (two after the break) and half a minute for each goal, card and penalty in the half, at most six. The whistle then waits, for at most four seconds, while a shot is on its way or a man is through on goal with the ball.
- **Fewer than seven (Law 3).** A side reduced to six by red cards has the match abandoned, awarded 3–0 against it (or the score, if the other side was already further ahead).
- **Offside (Law 11).** Judged when a teammate plays the ball: in the opponents' half, nearer their goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent (level is onside). It is an offence only when that player then plays the ball; a deliberate play by a defender, or an onside teammate getting it first, wipes it. A teammate's header, deflection or won tackle plays the ball too, so positions are judged again then; a defender's deflection or a save does not. No offside from a throw-in, goal kick or corner. An indirect free kick to the defenders where he touched it; the assistant's flag is an event.
- **Fouls and misconduct (Law 12).** A challenge that misses the ball, comes from behind, is late (the ball has gone) or goes in at full speed can be a foul: careless, reckless or (rarely) excessive. A direct free kick, or a penalty if it was inside the offender's own penalty area. The referee may play **advantage** when the fouled side keeps a good attack going (both arms out in front), and brings the free kick back if they lose the ball within two seconds, before they have had a shot, completed a pass or carried it into the box (FREE KICK, "advantage brought back", and a whistle). A late challenge on a player who has just shot never cancels the shot: it plays out, a goal stands, and if it doesn't go in the free kick or penalty for the challenge is given once it is over; no card for denying a goal-scoring opportunity, since the shot was had. A keeper may handle a teammate's header back to him; only a deliberate kick or a throw-in is a back-pass. Cards: yellow for a reckless challenge or for stopping a promising attack; red for excessive force or denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity (in the penalty area a genuine attempt at the ball is a yellow instead); a second yellow is a red. A player sent off leaves the pitch and his side plays on a man short (a keeper is replaced in goal by an outfielder). CPU players on a yellow tackle more carefully, and a CPU defender rarely goes in on a man with his back to him (more often only deep in his own third). Measured over 120 CPU matches: about 4 fouls, 0.7 yellows and 0.03 reds a match of 2 × 3 minutes.
- **Throw-ins and goal kicks.** A click on a team-mate throws it to him; if it would be led to where a marker is standing, it goes to his feet instead (with a marker close by, the human keeps 19 of 32 such throws in midfield, against 11 before). Hold left (or right) for a **long throw**: it goes where the button went down, up to 18 m plus up to 14 m more for a good passer, and is meant for the team-mate nearest where it comes down. At a goal kick, **hold left where it should come down** and it lands there (within about 2 m), driven or floated by the hold, as a cross does; a click to a centre-back plays it short.
- **Corners.** **Hold left where the cross should come down** and it lands there (within about a metre), wherever that is: a short hold drives it in (about 4.5 m up), a long one floats it (about 8 m up, slower). **Drag sideways while you hold** to swing it in or out (up to about 6 m off the straight line, and it still lands on the spot); the dots show its flight. A man comes short to the flag for your corners (and a good side's), free until it is played since the defence must stand 9.15 m off; one defender comes out to him. The corner waits, up to about 5.5 s, for the attackers to get up: four or five stand a few metres out from their zones (near post, far post, penalty spot) and attack them as it is struck, each with a man marking him goal-side who stays at his shoulder while it is in the air and challenges for it (the attacker wins about one in three between equals; taken on the chest under a challenge, it often comes off him). The keeper comes for a floated ball into his six-yard box (his defenders leave it to him), not a driven one. The same crosses work from a free kick out wide. Measured over 200 CPU matches, 4.5% of corners lead to a goal within five seconds (main: 6.7%).
- **Free kicks and penalties.** Direct or indirect: the HUD names which (an indirect one in yellow, "a goal needs a second touch", until someone else has touched it) and the referee keeps an arm up for an indirect one (Law 5). Within 32 m of goal the defenders build a wall, two to four men shoulder to shoulder 9.15 m from the ball covering the near post, before the kick is ready, also for an indirect kick; the keeper stands off the far side of it, the defence holds a line six metres in front of the ball (11 to 17 m out) with the attackers on it, and the kicking side keeps out of its own shooting lane. The wall stands still until the kick and jumps together as it is struck (its feet about 0.45 m up, so a ball driven low can go under it). Your shot from a free kick goes over the wall and dips in under the bar with topspin, to where right went down; **drag the pointer sideways while you hold right** to bend it that way (about 4 m of pitch for full curl), and the dots show its real flight. Pace is the gamble: a quick click is the fastest strike that still dips in, a harder one beats the keeper sooner but too hard sails over the bar. Over the ball before the wall is set, a click takes a **quick free kick** at once. The CPU takes them the same way. Measured from 22 m (60 kicks each): a click at the near top corner scores about 20% and almost never hits the wall (0 to 5%, against 23 to 80% on the old shots), the far top corner needs curl (12% with, 0% without), and full power sails over about 80% of the time. Wide ones are crossed into the box, with the defence marking. A penalty is taker against keeper on his line, everyone else outside the area and the arc. It is the shootout's kick (`src/sim/penalty.ts`): where you aim across the goal and how hard (the harder, the higher it comes in; past the sweet spot it may go over) against the keeper's dive, which he commits to as it is struck, decide it, and the ball and the keeper then do what was decided (a save can still be followed up). The CPU picks its kick as it does in a shootout. Measured: placed by the post at sweet-spot power about 82-85% go in (they used to almost all go in), down the middle a quarter to a half are saved (the softer, the likelier), and the CPU scores about 75% (71% in a shootout; the rest are rebounds).
- **The referee** runs the diagonal, 10 to 20 m from the ball. Whether a foul is given, and which card, are seeded rolls weighted by how bad the challenge was and how far away he is, so matches and replays stay deterministic.

Every whistle is in `MatchState.events` with its tick, match clock, team and player: goals, fouls, cards, sendings-off, offsides, restarts awarded and taken, advantage, half-time and full-time. `s.restart`, `s.referee`, `s.cards` and each player's `yellow` / `sentOff` describe the state for renderers.

Stats (`s.stats`): possession counts live-ball ticks only (no dead balls), to the side that last controlled the ball, including while it travels between teammates; a long dribble is possession too. A pass counts when a teammate takes it, whether it went along the ground, in the air, from a throw-in or from the keeper; `passAttempts` counts every one played. A shot is a deliberate strike at goal from within 35 m; a lofted ball or header only counts when it would cross the line near the frame. The result screen's old "82% possession, 8 passes" came from lofted passes (which slow clicks often became) never counting as passes, and from the ball in flight between teammates counting for nobody, so a side that dribbles looked far more dominant than one that passes.

### The 3D view

Matches, hot-seat, linked matches and the replay viewer draw the match in 3D with three.js (r186, vendored in `vendor/three/`, no npm). It is an afternoon match in a small stadium: mown grass, markings built from geometry, goals whose nets bulge and ripple, ad boards, stands with an instanced crowd waving flags in the home colours (the away end in theirs), orange floodlight pylons, and a car park, trees and a low town outside.

- **Players** are procedural low-poly footballers: each is one skinned mesh (a 14-bone skeleton, every primitive baked into one geometry with vertex colours, so a player is one draw call) in the team's shirt, shorts and socks, with a number on the back, a keeper in a contrasting kit (the away side changes to its swapped or a neutral kit when the shirts would be hard to tell apart under normal, protan or deutan colour vision: `src/client/kits.ts`; the keeper's kit is picked the same way, and against the grass), a dark rim round the silhouette for a kit too close to the grass as drawn (shirts are compared with the grass as the 3D view lights and tone-maps them, a model fitted to screenshots of every ladder shirt by `scripts/kit-probe.cjs`), shirt-coloured dots over the players when they are drawn too small to read (under 11 px tall, as in the long view on an upright phone: `src/client/r3d/dots3d.ts`), and varied skin tones, hair colours and styles, height and build. Animation is code-driven from the sim: idle, walk, jog and sprint blended by speed with arm swing and a stride that grows with pace, backpedal and side-step; pass, lofted ball and shot (striking foot picked by direction), standing tackle and slide tackle, being knocked down, headers and jumps for high balls, keeper set stance (also on his line for a penalty, eyes on the ball while he shuffles across), dive (left or right, by height, lying out on the grass until the sim lets him up), catch and holding the ball, rolling it out underarm, throw-in hold and throw, goal celebrations (arm punched high, aeroplane, knee slide), teammates cheering and opponents with hands on heads. `src/client/r3d/player3d.ts` exports a `Player3D` (`update(dt, simPlayer, events, ctx)`), so a rigged glTF character could replace the procedural one.
- **Camera:** an elevated broadcast view from the long side (33 degrees down, about 48 m of pitch across at the ball on a desktop window, about 45 m on a phone in portrait) that follows the ball through a small dead zone. The pointer's spot on the grass is taken when you move the mouse and kept while it is still, so your player runs to that spot and stops there even as the camera follows him, and widens a little when the ball is in the air; a lower, closer shot for goal celebrations. **Zoom** with the mouse wheel over the pitch (down shows more pitch; zoom follows how far you scroll, so a trackpad zooms smoothly, and a trackpad pinch over the pitch zooms the camera rather than the page; a mouse wheel with Ctrl held still zooms the page), **+** / **−** (**0** resets), or on a touch screen a pinch or the **−** / **+** buttons, from about 29 to 69 m across; it is remembered in this browser (`localStorage['derby-zoom']`). When your side has a goal kick or a free kick in its own half, or your keeper holds the ball, the camera eases up and out to a long view framed to keep the ball, both touchlines and 20 m beyond halfway in shot on any screen shape, and stays up for as long as the restart waits for you, and eases back about a second after the kick. The small **Cam** chip (top right) or **V** switches to an end-to-end view from behind your own goal, looking up the pitch at the goal you attack (WASD then follows the screen: W is always toward their goal). Hot-seat always uses the side view. With `prefers-reduced-motion` the camera moves more slowly, does not swoop in for celebrations, and the screen never shakes.
- **HUD:** crests, codes and score top left with the match clock (MM:SS of 90 minutes) under it; Esc / Menu and the mode top right; big words for GOAL, HALF TIME, FULL TIME, REPLAY (and OFFSIDE, FOUL when the sim reports them); a radar bottom centre (home dots, away triangles that point where they face); the shot power bar bottom right.
- **Instant replays:** after a goal, a card, a save from a shot on target, a shot off the woodwork or a near miss (within 0.5 m of a post or the bar), the next stoppage (or your keeper holding the ball) shows a 4 to 7 s replay: a low side camera, then (except for cards) from behind the goal for the half-speed last 1.5 s, with REPLAY at the top. The live match is held while it plays and resumes on the tick it stopped at. Click, Space, Enter or Esc skips it (in hot-seat, where Space and Enter pass, only a click or Esc); the radar and clock follow the replay; at most one per stoppage, other than goals and red cards at most one every 90 s of play, never in a two-tab match, and the pause menu turns them off (`localStorage['derby-instant-replays']`). The last 12 s of the match are kept as half-second snapshots plus the inputs, and a replay re-simulates from the snapshot (`src/client/highlights.ts`). With `prefers-reduced-motion` they are off until turned on in the pause menu.
- The renderer reads the sim only. Pitch, goal and team size come from `src/sim/constants.ts` and the state, so a bigger 11-a-side pitch works; if the state has `restart` (a throw-in taker holds the ball overhead), `referee` (drawn in black) or card events (the referee holds the card up, the card shows over the player), they are drawn.
- **Fallback and quality:** without WebGL2 the old 2D canvas renderer is used. Software rasterisers (SwiftShader, llvmpipe) get a low-quality mode (no shadow maps, blob shadows, a thinner crowd, no town, 0.8 resolution). `?renderer=2d|3d` or `localStorage['derby-renderer']` forces a renderer, `?quality=low|high` or `localStorage['derby-quality']` the quality. The training ground tutorial still uses the 2D view.

## Playing the CPU

Every match is the same length: 2 × 3 minutes (`HALF_SECONDS` in `src/sim/constants.ts`). There is no length setting; `prefs.halfSeconds` in `src/client/store.ts` is only a hidden override the e2e tests use for short matches.

- **Ranked** (Home, **Kick off**): one match against a ladder club near your Elo, from any division. Moves Elo. The opponent is fixed once offered (a reload shows the same club). Money and XP scale with the opponent: its division (×1 to ×3 money, ×1 to ×1.75 XP) and 0.7 to 1.3 by how unlikely a win was, so beating weak clubs pays less.
- **Practice** (Home, Practice card): Easy, Normal or Hard CPU. No Elo, no money, no calendar week. It pays a flat 30, 40 or 60 XP and a training session, develops the players who play, refreshes the transfer market, and is written to the match history and news.
- **Cup** (Competitions tab): 8-club knockout, you plus four clubs from your division and three from the division above (in the top division, the other seven), seeded 1v8, 4v5, 2v7, 3v6. Quarter-final, semi-final, final; a draw you play goes to a live penalty shootout (best of five, then sudden death; Law 10: only the players on the pitch at full time kick, the keeper in goal at the end keeps goal, the side with more players leaves out its weakest shooters to match, and everyone kicks once, the keeper last, before anyone kicks twice): as taker, aim with the mouse, hold the left button for power and release (too hard skies it, corners are riskier); as keeper, read the run-up and click left, middle or right to dive. Arrow keys and Space work too, and leaving counts as a loss. Ties between two CPU clubs use a seeded roll weighted by shooting and goalkeeping. Prize money for every round reached; a win goes in the trophy cabinet.
A new cup can start once you have played 4 ranked or league matches since the last one started.
- **League** (Competitions tab): your division's 8 clubs, single round robin over 7 matchdays, 3/1/0 points, then goal difference, then goals scored. Prize money by finishing place and division, paid for the matchdays you play. The top two go up a division and the bottom two go down (a season with more than two forfeits can't win a title or promotion).

**The world.** 32 clubs in four divisions of eight (`src/meta/world.ts`); you start in Division 4. CPU squads are the same players season after season: they age, retire, bring through academy youngsters and move up in a transfer window, and clubs develop toward their division's level. The top division rises with your XI, so the best clubs stay a match for you. At each season rollover the CPU divisions play a modelled season and two clubs go up and two down at each boundary; the season review lists the champions, moves, transfers and notable retirements.

You play your own cup ties and league fixtures live. The other matches in the round get a full-length scoreline from the two XIs (a Poisson model, about 2.6 goals between equal sides; `quickScore` in world.ts), so results follow the squads. Competition matches pay money, training and player development and move Elo (yours and the opponent's), but not the ladder record. Forfeiting one counts as 0–3. A rated forfeit (ranked, cup or league) also costs a fine of what a win would have paid (what the bank can't cover is owed, from half of later income) and a win's XP, owed from the next rated matches (half of each at most), so losing Elo on purpose to meet weaker clubs never pays. A ranked, cup or league match left mid-way (a reload, a closed tab) is recorded as a 0–3 forfeit the next time the game opens, with a message that says so; if the game itself crashed during it, it is voided instead, at most once in 10 matches. Friendlies record nothing.

CPU difficulty is `TeamSetup.aiSkill` (0..1, and down to −0.3 for a rookie side). It sets reaction time, decision noise, execution error and how well the team defends (marking zone, interceptions, a sweeper, the keeper's sweeping); see the header of `src/sim/ai.ts`.

### Fair matches

One person steers one player among 22, so two things decide whether a match is fair: what the other ten do around them, and how sharp the CPU opposite is.

- **Teammates cover for you.** When your man isn't going in on the carrier (too far, or running elsewhere), the next-best teammate presses or chases the loose ball for you, instead of nobody. If you give no input at all for 0.75 s (in mouse mode, the pointer not on the pitch), or right after the game switches you to a new man and before you move the pointer, your man holds his place in the shape, marks, and jockeys a carrier who comes at him from 2 m goal-side. He never tackles for you. When you take your man out of his zone, a free teammate picks up the attackers in it. Defending, the automatic switch gives you the man who can press from goal-side, not one chasing back behind the ball. A pointer resting on the pitch is always a command ("run there and stay"), and so is the keyboard with no key down ("stand still"): neither is treated as idle. Code: `humanEngaging`, `humanAuto` and the plan in `src/sim/ai.ts`; `jockey` and `updateControl` in `src/sim/human.ts`.
- **Difficulty that fits you.** Every club carries a hidden skill estimate, separate from Elo (`src/meta/skill.ts`). It learns from every match against the CPU, fast at first, then steadier. For ranked and cup or league matches it sets the opposing CPU's `aiSkill` within a band: from 0.75 below the club's natural skill (no lower than a rookie side) to 0.25 above it. The aim is a 60% chance of a win for you (about a quarter of matches are drawn, so that is a fair fight with a little in your favour). The ladder club's squad, shape and Elo are never touched, so Elo stays the honest ladder. Practice levels stay fixed as labelled. The **win chance** on Home is the same model's chance against the opponent as he will really play you (`winChance`). Its constants are fitted to the fairness harness (`bun run fairness --calibrate`).

`bun run fairness` measures all of this. Scripted humans, from one who never touches the mouse to a sharp one, each start a new club and play it the way the game does: first match, warm-ups, then ranked against the offered opponent (`offeredOpponent`) with the CPU's skill from the estimate. Each then plays three friendlies against the top of the ladder. "Settled" means the ranked matches after the first three, once the estimate has had something to learn from. 12 careers of 12 matches per bot:

| Player | First 3 ranked: W-D-L, goal diff | All ranked: W-D-L | Won | Shown | Settled: won (shown) | CPU skill faced | vs top 3 clubs: won (shown) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Never touches the mouse | 0-26-10, −0.36 | 0-56-27 | 0% | 17% | 0% (11%) | −0.30 | 0% (0%) |
| Pointer on the ball, no clicks | 0-8-28, −2.06 | 0-15-58 | 0% | 12% | 0% (9%) | −0.30 | 0% (0%) |
| Chaser: runs at the ball, shoots early | 22-11-3, +1.03 | 61-26-17 | 59% | 57% | 57% (56%) | −0.02 | 3% (3%) |
| Competent: passes to open teammates, shoots in range | 17-9-10, +0.17 | 54-27-28 | 50% | 58% | 51% (57%) | 0.05 | 0% (2%) |
| Strong: the CPU's pass choice, patient tackles | 21-8-7, +0.56 | 65-24-27 | 56% | 62% | 55% (61%) | 0.36 | 14% (8%) |
| (The same club run by the CPU) | 18-13-5, +1.06 | 63-37-17 | 54% | 61% | 56% (60%) | 0.30 | 11% (9%) |

The top three clubs are the first division's best, so even the CPU running a new club's squad rarely beats them.

Without the band, at a CPU skill of 0.6 against every formation and mentality, the tactics stream's probe (`bun run scripts/offball-probe.ts 32 --human`, 416 matches) shows what the cover alone does. On main 50b7a1c: 0.40 goals for, 4.34 against, 2.7 shots to 22.6. On this branch: 0.44 for, 4.26 against, 2.8 shots to 21.5. Earlier, on main 8e4ce9f, `bun run fairness 24 --tiers=first` (before → after) showed the same: the cover fixes the shape (the carrier pressed by nobody, with no CPU presser either, fell from 45-61% of the time to 0-34%; shots against fell for every bot). Goals move within the noise, about ±0.3 a match. What makes matches winnable is the difficulty band. `bun run fairness 12 --assert` fails CI if any of these break:

- a passive human is routed (goal difference worse than −3 in the first ranked matches);
- the competent bot is far from an even chance (outside 40-65% wins);
- the strong bot doesn't win most of its ranked matches, or doesn't win clearly fewer against the top clubs;
- once settled, the chance shown drifts from the chance measured beyond sampling noise.

The estimate learns from wins, since a win is what the gauge shows. A player who never shoots draws 0–0 often but never wins. His estimate sinks with every match he doesn't win, but each step is sized by the chance he was shown, so the smaller it gets the slower it falls: about 11% once settled, and toward 0 over later matches. It can't drop faster without making the estimate jumpy for everyone who does play. Before it has learned, a new player is shown a newcomer's chance.

Every finished match leaves a replay: the match options plus the per-tick input log (run-length and delta encoded, cursor to 1 cm, then compressed with a small adaptive range coder, `src/meta/pack.ts`), which the deterministic sim re-runs exactly. A full match is a code of about 45-70K characters. **Watch goals** on the result dialog plays each goal's last six seconds (½× slow-mo, previous/next goal, scrub bar; Space, arrows and Esc work). The last five replays are kept in this browser under **Recent replays** on the Club tab (fewer only if they are unusually long), and **Copy replay code** / **Paste replay code** share one as an `OVR1.…` string. Scrubbing a whole match is held at the part already prepared while the rest loads. Code is in `src/meta/replay.ts` (pure) and `src/client/replayView.ts`.

A first visit is one welcome with one big **Kick off** (a small "I've played before" skips it): no forms, no controls quiz. You are playing your first match, a friendly against a generated easy club, within one click. Light coach tips teach inside it (`src/client/coach.ts`): how to run (the pointer distance), the first time you have the ball (click to pass), near goal (right click to shoot), losing the ball (right click to tackle) and your first restart. Each tip shows once, ever, is dismissible, fades by itself, never pauses play and is announced to screen readers. Afterwards a short celebration of the result and the first XP (a "first match" bonus makes level 2 certain), then an optional, skippable step to name the club and pick colours. The training-ground drills are the optional **Practice skills** button in Club news (Club tab, unlocked at level 3). If you leave or reload the first match the welcome comes back with the same opponent; the first match counts once it is played to the end, not a gate; they earn their one-time XP there. Players who already had a save skip all of this, and see no tips or marks.

## Home

The first screen does one job. The hero is the next ranked match against the CPU: both crests, Elo numbers, your last five results as W/D/L chips (and a small Elo sparkline once there is history), a win-chance gauge and one big **Kick off**. Under it sit the play-mode cards: **vs Player**, **Practice** (pick the CPU difficulty, each button starts a match) and **Cup** and **League**, shown with a padlock and their level until they open. The strip at the top holds the club, its level and XP bar, and gains training sessions (pips), money and the ladder position as those features unlock. News, replays, the club level panel, the tutorial and reset are on the **Club** tab. Code: `src/client/home.ts`; the strip is `statusStrip` in `src/client/ui.ts`.

## Club levels

The club earns experience and opens up as it levels (1 to 99; clearing level L takes 100 × L XP). A new club has Home with one Kick off and nothing else (no tabs). The menus are revealed one step per level: level 2 adds the Squad, vs Player (Local 2P and Link two tabs) and Easy practice; level 3 Ranked matches, the Club tab, replays and Practice skills; 4 Training and Practice Normal; 5 the Cup and Practice Hard; 6 Transfers; 7 the Ladder; 8 the League; 10 the season calendar and academy; then small perks up to 99. Until level 3, Kick off is a warm-up friendly against a gentle generated club (no Elo, still XP), and the Elo win-chance gauge is hidden for the first three ranked matches. Each newly revealed tab gets a spotlight and a small card once (`pendingMark` in `src/client/coach.ts`), after the level-up dialog; opening the tab counts as seeing it. A save from before this table (`unlocksV` missing) opens each feature at the earlier of its old and new level, so nothing it was working toward moves away. Matches pay XP (win 120, draw 80, loss 50, +10 a goal, +20 a clean sheet; practice 30 to 60), so do training sessions (+5), cup and league finishes and first-time milestones. Forfeits pay nothing and cost a win's XP from the next matches. The header shows the level and XP bar; the Club level panel on the Club tab lists every unlock. Older saves get XP back from their match history and keep anything they already use. The full tables and pacing are under "Club levels" in the roadmap; the rules are in `src/meta/levels.ts`.

## Two players

Both are on Home in the **vs Player** card (**One screen** is Local 2P, **Two tabs** is Link two tabs, and **Online** when a relay is configured), open from club level 2. Each human steers one player of their side (yellow marker for home, cyan for away); the CPU runs the rest of both teams. Nothing is recorded: no Elo, money, training or replay.

- **Local 2P:** hot-seat on one keyboard, your club against a ladder club you pick.

  | | Player 1 (home, yellow) | Player 2 (away, cyan) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Move | W A S D, or the mouse once you move it over the pitch (further = faster) | Arrow keys |
  | Pass / tackle | Space, or left click / right click | Enter or `/` |
  | Lofted ball | Hold C, release (or hold left button) | Hold `M`, release |
  | Shoot | Hold F, release (or right button) | Hold `.` (period), release |
  | Switch player | E, or left click a player | `,` (comma) |
  | Sprint | Left Shift (the mouse sprints by distance) | Right Shift |
  | Pause | Esc or P | Esc or P |

- **Link two tabs:** open `dist/index.html` in two tabs or windows of the same browser. Your club can only be played in one tab (the second shows "Derby Day is open in another tab"), so in the second tab choose **Use as a guest tab**: it plays Link matches but saves nothing. In one, **Host a match** and read out the 4-letter code; in the other, type it and **Join**. Each tab plays its own side with the normal controls. The tabs run the same match in deterministic lockstep over `BroadcastChannel` (no server) with a 4-tick (67 ms) input delay, exchange only inputs, and compare a state hash every second; a mismatch stops both with "Desync at tick N". Sandboxed viewers (such as some artifact hosts) block tabs from talking; the lobby says so. See "Multiplayer spike" in the roadmap for what this proves.
- **Online:** two browsers anywhere, over a small WebSocket relay (`server/`). **Create a match** shows a 6-letter code and an invite link (`https://derby.natwong.dev/?join=CODE`); the friend opens the link and is in. Same lockstep match as two tabs. A dropped connection reconnects and resumes the seat; while the opponent is gone the match pauses with "Opponent disconnected, waiting 20 s", then ends as an abandoned friendly. The button only appears when the build has a relay URL (`DERBY_RELAY_URL`, empty by default) or a local page has `?relay=ws://...` (ignored on the real site). Run one locally with `bun server/bun.ts` and open `dist/index.html?relay=ws://localhost:8787/ws`. Architecture, limits, threat model and deploy steps: [docs/ONLINE.md](docs/ONLINE.md). To deploy the relay on Fly.io, fill in the two placeholders in `fly.toml` and follow [Deploy on Fly.io](docs/ONLINE.md#deploy-on-flyio).

## Saves, backups and going live

The club lives in this browser's localStorage. One tab owns it at a time; each save keeps the previous good one as a backup and a damaged save is restored from it; a full disk shows a message instead of losing progress. **Club → Back up or restore** exports the club as a file or a copyable code and imports it back after a preview. Crashes show a calm "Something went wrong" panel, the page installs as an app and opens offline after one visit, and telemetry and crash reporting are compiled out unless `DERBY_TELEMETRY_URL` / `DERBY_ERROR_URL` are set at build time. How deploys and rollbacks work, what is collected (nothing by default), and the launch checklist are in [docs/OPS.md](docs/OPS.md).

### End-to-end tests

`tests/e2e/` holds browser tests run by a small dependency-free runner (`tests/e2e/run.cjs`). They open `dist/index.html` in Chromium and exercise boot, every tab at 1280 and 390 px in light and dark, training, buying and selling, lineup persistence, a short ranked match, pause, forfeit and keyboard-only play.

Playwright is not a repo dependency. Install it once globally and let Node find it:

```sh
npm i -g playwright@1 && playwright install chromium
NODE_PATH="$(npm root -g)" bun run e2e
```

The runner also falls back to the global npm root and `/opt/node22/lib/node_modules` on its own, so `NODE_PATH` is usually optional. Set `PLAYWRIGHT_CHROMIUM=/path/to/chrome` to use a specific browser (default: `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium` if it exists, otherwise Playwright's own). `node tests/e2e/run.cjs <text>` runs only tests whose name contains `<text>`; `E2E_HEADED=1` shows the browser.

The runner never hangs silently: a test that times out prints each open page's state (what it shows, `navigator.locks.query()`), a page whose renderer crashes fails its test at once, and closing a test's browser contexts is bounded (CDP throttle sessions are detached and pages closed one by one first; otherwise `context.close()` stalled about one run in five under `E2E_CPU_THROTTLE`). `E2E_TIMEOUT_MS` and `E2E_CLOSE_TIMEOUT_MS` change the limits. A test's verdict is in before teardown, so pages that take longer than `E2E_PAGE_CLOSE_MS` (1.5 s) to close are not waited for: the runner kills its browser and starts a fresh one (about 0.3 s; on CI a busy 3D page under SwiftShader took 5–20 s to close).

CI runs the suite as six parallel jobs (`E2E_SHARD=k/6`), split by test, not by file, and balanced by each test's recorded seconds in `tests/e2e/shard-weights.json`. Every job uploads its timings as an `e2e-timings-<k>` artifact; when a job warns that its shard is stale (or after adding slow tests), download a run's artifacts and run `node tests/e2e/weights.cjs e2e-timings-*.json` to refresh the file.

The unit tests split the same way across two CI jobs, by file (`bun test $(bun run scripts/unit-shard.ts 1/2)`), balanced by `tests/unit-weights.json`; `bun run scripts/unit-shard.ts --measure` re-times every file and rewrites it.

Locally, `E2E_JOBS=3 bun run e2e` runs three shards side by side, each with its own browser: the whole suite in about 13 min on a 4-core machine instead of about 25. The 3D views share the CPU then (SwiftShader draws in software), so a test that measures real-time pace can fail under that load (the replay viewer's "about 60 ticks a second" did once in such a run); re-run one with `node tests/e2e/run.cjs <name>` before trusting the failure.

Tests that only need a match played out, to check what follows it (the result, XP, levels, the season review, the recorded replay), pass `fast: true` to `openApp` (or call `fastMatches(context)`): the page sets `window.__e2eSpeed` and the match runs 8× faster than real time, with the same ticks and inputs, so the same result and replay. Tests of the match itself, its timing or its controls play at real speed, and `match.e2e.cjs` keeps one short match at real speed through to the result.

All selectors live in `tests/e2e/lib/helpers.cjs` (`SEL`), including the best-effort onboarding skipper. When the UI changes, fix them there.
